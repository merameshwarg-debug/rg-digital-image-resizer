What is DPI?

DPI stands for Dots Per Inch. It is commonly used to describe the resolution or print density of an image. Different printing and document requirements may specify different DPI values.

Common DPI Values

DPI Common Use
72 DPI Basic web and screen use
96 DPI Digital documents and screens
150 DPI Basic document printing
200 DPI Good quality document printing
300 DPI High-quality printing and documents
600 DPI High-resolution scanning and detailed printing

Important: Changing the DPI metadata alone does not automatically create more image detail. The actual pixel dimensions and original image quality are also important for printing quality.

300 DPI Image

300 DPI is commonly associated with high-quality printing. If a particular application or printing service specifically requires 300 DPI, follow its stated requirements for both DPI and image dimensions.

200 DPI Image

200 DPI can be suitable for some document and printing requirements. Always check the exact specifications provided by the organization or application.

150 DPI Image

150 DPI may be suitable for basic document printing where extremely high print resolution is not required.
